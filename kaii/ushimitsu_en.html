In Edo-period Japan, hours were not fixed. The night — sunset to sunrise — was divided into six temporal hours; the fifth is the Hour of the Ox (ushi no koku). That hour is split into four quarters, and the third quarter is ushimitsu-doki: the true Japanese witching hour, when spirits are said to walk. Because the length of night changes with season and latitude, ushimitsu shifts every single night. Solar positions are computed astronomically on your device.
